About Flo

Flo is a comprehensive, AI-powered health app designed to empower women at every stage of their reproductive journey, from their first menstruation to menopause. With its primary functionalities including personalised cycle predictions, fertility and general health insights, and community-driven discussions, Flo breaks taboos while fostering open dialogue. Backed by medical professionals, the app delivers evidence-based health information while prioritising user privacy and security.

Offering tailored experiences based on user goals—such as cycle tracking, pregnancy planning, or health monitoring—Flo provides both premium and free versions. Features like symptom tracking, health insights, and interactive graphs enhance the user experience. Trusted by over 380 million users worldwide, Flo has become a go-to platform for women to better understand and manage their health.


Core Value Proposition (CVP):

Empowering women at every stage of their reproductive journey through personalized health insights, cycle tracking, and community-driven support.

  • Cycle Tracking & Planning: Flo simplifies cycle management with AI-powered predictions, helping women feel confident about their health.
  • Breaking Taboos: Through anonymous conversations and community discussions, Flo encourages open communication about sensitive health topics.
  • Health Empowerment: By combining medical expertise with an intuitive interface, Flo equips women with the tools to understand their bodies better and make informed decisions.

Flo is more than just a health tracker—it's a comprehensive platform for women’s health and well-being, seamlessly blending technology, privacy, and medically-backed insights into an experience that users trust and value.

Breadth as the Primary Framework for Flo

Why Breadth? Breadth in engagement framework is about leveraging the adjacent features of a product to enhance the overall experience of the core value proposition (CVP). Just like Apple’s ecosystem, where using multiple products (e.g., iPhone, Mac, and Apple Watch) amplifies the overall value, Flo’s ecosystem of features ensures that users gain a holistic and enriched experience when they use the product as a whole.

For Flo, users start with one primary feature—such as cycle tracking—but the true potential of the app is realised only when they also engage with complementary features like health insights, community discussions, symptom logging, and partner mode. These features work together to amplify the CVP and make Flo a comprehensive health solution rather than just a period tracker.


Depth as the Secondary Framework for Flo


Time Investment:
Depth measures the time users spend engaging with various features. This can include reading articles, interacting in the community, or analysing health reports.
By encouraging users to spend more time on high-value activities, Flo increases user satisfaction and perceived value, leading to better retention.

Multiplying Impact of Breadth:
As users explore more features (Breadth), they are likely to spend more time on each. For example, a user who only tracks periods may spend 5 minutes/month, while a user exploring symptom patterns and health insights may spend 30-40 minutes/month.


Converting to Premium:
Depth also drives revenue, as users who spend more time are more likely to see value in premium features like advanced reports or partner modes.


Now, why is Frequency not Selected as the engagement framework?


While maintaining regular usage is essential, Flo's primary actions (logging periods, tracking symptoms) are inherently tied to biological rhythms. Attempting to increase Frequency unnecessarily may feel unnatural or forced. Instead, focusing on how deeply users engage during their interactions makes more sense.

By analysing retention at key intervals (Day 1, Day 7, Day 30, etc.), we can uncover trends in user behaviour, pinpoint pain points, and highlight opportunities to improve engagement.

Understanding the 60% Average Retention Rate (as stated in the article)

The 60% retention rate mentioned in the article reflects Flo's ability to retain users in the long term. It likely refers to the proportion of users retained as "active users" over a defined period (e.g., after Day 90 or 180 or even more). It does not mean 60% of all users remain after Day 1 or Day 7, as retention usually drops steeply at the start.


To align this with the retention curve:

Cumulative Active Users: From 280M total registered users, there are 60M monthly active users (MAUs), indicating 21.4% remain engaged actively within a month. Monthly Active Users are a combination of:

  • Retained users (those who continuously use the app).
  • Returning users (those who were previously inactive but re-engage).
  • New users acquired during the month who stay active.
  1. Day-by-Day Retention (Short Term): Early drop-offs occur due to natural churn (e.g., Day 1 retention at 45%, Day 7 at 30% and Day 30 at 25%).
  2. Stabilization (Long Term): By Day 180, the curve flattens, representing users who consistently engage with Flo (loyal core and power users).
  3. Returning Users: The 60% includes periodic users who re-engage with the app due to features like cycle tracking, community engagement, or premium adoption.

Understanding Churn for Flo

Voluntary Churn 😒

  1. Fatigue from Manual Logging: Users feel burdened by repetitive logging tasks, especially if they do not see immediate value in the data logged.
  2. Over-Reliance on Free Version: Many users may find the free version sufficient and avoid upgrading to premium, leading to limited engagement with advanced features.
  3. Switching to Competitors: Users migrate to apps offering better community features, more engaging UI, or broader health and wellness solutions.
  4. Low Perceived Value of Premium Features: Users may not find enough differentiation between free and premium plans to justify the upgrade.
  5. Compatibility with Ecosystems: Transition to apps that integrate better with their health ecosystems (e.g., Apple Health for device syncing).

Involuntary Churn 😢

  1. Health Milestones or Transitions: Users entering menopause, postpartum, or stable fertility periods may feel less need for tracking.
  2. Seasonal or Occasional Users: Some users engage only during certain life phases, like pregnancy planning or early cycles, and churn once these needs are met.
  3. Poor Onboarding Completion Rates: New users who fail to navigate the onboarding process or don’t log data within the first session often churn early.

Negative Actions to Look Out For

For All Users

  • Decline in Logging Activities: Users logging less frequently over time or skipping data altogether.
  • Dismissed Notifications: Increased frequency of ignored prompts for logging, insights, or engagement with community features.
  • Reduced App Session Time: Shorter visits and fewer daily or weekly interactions with the app.
  • Complaints or Feedback on Features: Recurring mentions of missing functionality, poor UX, or unmet expectations.
  • Low Feature Utilisation: Users who stop exploring new or premium features entirely.

Power Users Slipping to Core Users 🔥 → 🌟

  • Fewer Log Entries: Power users reduce the number of daily or weekly inputs for symptoms, cycle data, or reports.
  • Skipping Advanced Features: Less engagement with webinars, health insights, or personalised reports.
  • Ignoring Notifications: Previously responsive users start disregarding partner updates, cycle insights, or new content notifications.

Core Users Slipping to Casual Users 🌟 → 🌼

  • Minimal Interaction with Premium Features: Logging period dates without delving into symptom tracking or fertility tools.
  • Reduced Learning Engagement: Ignoring health reports or educational articles, even during critical phases like pregnancy tracking.
  • App Avoidance: Rarely opening the app except for specific notifications or reminders.

Casual Users Slipping to Churn 🌼 → 😢

  • No Consistent Logging: Sporadic entries that become increasingly rare over time.
  • Avoiding Notifications and Offers: Persistent dismissal of prompts to log data or explore premium features.
  • No Engagement with Onboarding Features: Users fail to progress past initial setup and log entries, especially within the first week.


These detailed approaches ensures Flo can pinpoint key reasons for churn and address them effectively to boost retention and long-term engagement.
