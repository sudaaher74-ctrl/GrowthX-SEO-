Product Engagement

Product Hooks

  1. Problem: Dark Web Monitoring is a feature that has a very low natural frequency and the value prop cannot be realized by the user unless there’s an incidental breach.

Goal

User finds out Dark Web Monitoring is not just monitoring the PII they entered, but at relevant, contextual Moment of Needs. 

Success Metric

User either/and sets up Password manager and/or Dark Web Monitoring from these hooks | User visits Dark Web Monitoring feature from this hook | Makes a core/casual user to a Power user. 

Current Alternative

Google search, just “knowing” the website is breached, changing passwords for ALL their accounts every week.

Metrics to track

# of times a website was detected as safe vs. not safe, # of times user hovered over the banner, open rate of the banner, # of times dark web feature was setup, # times password manager was setup, # times user lands to either or both feature if they had already set it up and made a change.

Ramp up milestones

​Since its not a new functionality but just re-purposing an existing one with a new use case: 

  1. Crawl: Start showing only the banner to customers with details.
  2. Walk: Show the banner to users with a CTA to take them to each of the respective features
  3. Run: Show the banner to users with a CTA that takes them directly to the relevant section in each feature. EG: If insurance website is breached, take them to the page in dark web monitoring where they can add their insurance account to monitor. 


NOTE: If customers have not setup Internet Security, this product hook will run only on converted customers from Engagement Campaign - 3.

Based on the pattern of usage and the timeline of curve flattening, and general usage of all features, Norton’s retention typically flattens by M6-M9 because while there are opportunities to create habit forming behaviours with the features available now, some of the other features like Antivirus has no scope and ends up having no value to our ICPs; same with features like Cloud backup. Additionally, time taken to “discover” each feature, even if valuable to customers is much longer and quite tedious.

As the customer reaches M10, they will be informed about Auto-Renewal (that is turned on by default).

​

​

​

But, what are the reasons for churn?

​

Voluntary Churn

  1. Continued value: The product could “usable” for one year but after that, it would fail to provide the value for money especially with Antivirus being part of price.
  2. Family sharing vs. # of devices: The limitation on # of devices is intended to limit usage of Antivirus, however if a spouse or another member would need to monitor their dark web or use password manager, they would need to share credentials.
  3. VPN is still advertised & accounted for in the price: VPN is restricted in India yet it is bundled with a plan and being marketed as “can be used while travelling outside India”. While this cannot be salvaged with ANY engagement/Retention campaign, Norton should just seperate it from the bundle and either provide it free or as a pay-as needed service.
  4. Lack of depth and range of features offered or to solve customer’s Privacy/Identity needs that could be a factor of local government regulations (Involuntary) or just business decisions in prioritizing to lead the core market (USA)
  5. Core competition on Identity & Privacy solutions: McAfee has higher chances to reach customers as they are partners with major laptop OEMs with each new laptop launching with a trial version of McAfee + ecosystems likes Apple prioritizing on Data Privacy based solutions.
  6. Some core features like Dark Web Monitoring only provide reactive steps with no concrete resolution (such as deleting information in the dark web), thereby leading to customers resigning to breaches occurring, leading to churn.

Involuntary Churn

  1. Changes in our target customers’ spending levels as a result of general economic conditions, inflationary pressures or other factors.
  2. Disruption by new, “Made in India” companies and/or changes in law/regulations such as the DPDPA Act ‘23, especially with the Government having access to Consumer data as per the bill.
  3. Customers not needed AV/Security products because they are not a top pain point + the ecosystem (Windows Defender, Apple doesn't require) offers to solve the problem.


​

Negative Actions to Look Out for

  1. Customers who were using Dark Web Monitoring and had breaches are not reviewing their breaches.
  2. Customers who have setup Password Manager are not using it to auto-login at all.
  3. Customers who have setup their child in parental control are not using it to monitoring their child(ren).
  4. Customers have not setup scheduled scan in Antivirus and/or have provided the necessary permissions to scan their device regularly.
  5. Product CSAT/NPS score, comments.
  6. Continued product bugs either from the core products themselves or from integrations, that are customer facing.
  7. Support tickets - Continued increase by category of issues customer has called in for, type of issues report, support CSAT, Customer Effort Score, high average talk time for calls, high waiting times, customers reading less FAQ articles and calling support more.
  8. Negative Social media engagement.
  9. Metrics defined in Casual-Core-Power framework are under the defined thresholds for more than 90 days.

​

Microscopic view

  • Which ICPs drive the best Retention? 🚀

ICP 3 (Luke Skywalker) drives the best retention because:

  1. This ICP prioritizes time > money spent and does not expect “quick solutions”, which the core features of the product do not exhibit.
  2. The most common features that this ICP engages with are Password Manager, Parental Contol that solve a very specific use case both at high frequences & with a larger breadth.
  3. Once these customers experience the Core Value Proposition of the features, they will continue to be loyal to the brand and will also spread word of mouth.
  • Which channels drive the best Retention? 📡

Users who were acquired & activated from search organic channels, especially through expert reviews in YouTube are expected to drive the best retention because they were acquired through positive reviews from established tech-individuals and had intentionally searched for a specific use case/use case topic.

Users who have engaged with mobile devices more & who received campaigns through mobile are expected to retain more because the feature-wise engagement, interoperability are more robust & has less effort in the mobile app.

  • What sub-features or sub-products drive the best retention? 🔍

The features that drive the best retention, in order, are:

  1. Password Manager: The feature that is rich in addressing use cases across password management, security & interoperability with features like dark web monitoring that exhibits the core value proposition both frequently & for a longer time in a customer’s lifecycle.
  2. Parental Control: Customers (especially Core, Power) using Parental Control use the feature to not just protect themselves, but the people/children in their family, and thus drives more engagement & retention within a customer’s lifecycle.
  3. Dark Web Monitoring: Retention for Dark Web Monitoring is going to be much lesser in the first year vs. subsequent years because the topic of “Dark Web Breaches” are still not predominantly known to a large segment of consumers.
